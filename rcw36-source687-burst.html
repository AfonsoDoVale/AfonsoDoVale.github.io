An accretion-burst candidate in RCW 36

A source that became much fainter—and slightly bluer—across two decades of near-infrared observations.

While comparing RCW 36 observations taken over roughly two decades, we noticed something striking: source 687 was much brighter in the early SOFI images than in the later NACO, F2 and HAWK-I data. The plotted bright–faint differences are about 5.1 magnitudes in J and 1.6–1.7 magnitudes in H and Ks.

A clue in the colours

The source also tends to be redder when brighter, moving in a different direction from the extinction vector in the colour–magnitude diagram. This reminded me of the variability diagnostics I used in my Taurus master’s work, and led us to consider an accretion burst.

It is an intriguing candidate, rather than a confirmed eruption: 1–2 magnitudes of K-band variability can have several causes, and the gaps between observations leave the timescale unclear. If it belongs to RCW 36, it could be a brown dwarf or a very-low-mass star.

A source worth another look

In our discussions with Koraljka Mužić and Hervé Bouy, we considered deeper VISTA measurements and archival SINFONI spectroscopy as possible next steps. For me, it is a lovely connection between RCW 36 and young-object variability—and a starting point for exploring variable sources across the Vela Molecular Ridge.
