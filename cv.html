Research profile

I am a PhD researcher in Physics and Astrophysics at the University of Lisbon, working between the Instituto de Astrofísica e Ciências do Espaço in Lisbon and the Laboratoire d’Astrophysique de Bordeaux, supported by an FCT co-tutelle PhD grant.

My research explores the origin of brown dwarfs and free-floating planetary-mass objects. I am particularly interested in Galactic star formation, the initial mass function, and the disks and variability of young, low-mass objects. My PhD is supervised by Koraljka Mužić and Hervé Bouy.

Before my PhD, I studied Physics at the University of Lisbon, specialising in Astronomy and Astrophysics during my BSc and in Astrophysics and Cosmology during my MSc. My master’s thesis investigated the long-term variability of young stars and brown dwarfs in Taurus.

A. R. G. do Brito do Vale, K. Mužić, H. Bouy, V. Almendros-Abad, A. Bayo, D. Capela, A. Scholz, A. Bik, G. Suárez, L. Cieza, K. Peña Ramírez, E. Bertin & R. Schödel

Astronomy & Astrophysics · 706, A149 · First-author publication

Journal article ↗arXiv ↗
Research summary

A study of the initial mass function and substellar population of RCW 36, combining deep HAWK-I/VLT observations with archival photometry and Gaia kinematics. The analysis examines cluster membership, the low-mass population, and the ratio of stars to brown dwarfs.
