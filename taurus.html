Blinking Lights in Taurus

How much can we learn about a young star simply by watching its light change? For my master’s thesis, I assembled a sample of 491 spectroscopically confirmed Taurus members from the literature and studied their variability with ZTF optical and AllWISE mid-infrared photometry. Gaia astrometry adds context to this population spread across the Taurus molecular clouds.

Variability across the clouds

Combining several statistical variability indices, I identified 304 optical variables and 279 mid-infrared variables—about 79% and 62% of the samples with data in each survey. A total of 188 objects were selected in both. The ZTF and AllWISE observations cover different epochs, so I analysed their light curves separately before bringing the results together.

Bursts, dips and longer-term changes

The ZTF light curves reveal several kinds of behaviour, sometimes within the same source. Source 474 shows repeated brightenings, source 316 displays a deep dip, and source 56 gradually brightens over the observing baseline. These visual patterns help describe the variability before we investigate its physical origin.

Disks and changing classifications

Infrared colours help distinguish objects with different disk properties. Thick-disk sources dominate the classified sample, but a source’s classification is not necessarily fixed: 80 objects—32% of the subset examined for this—crossed a classification boundary during the AllWISE observations. Variability therefore matters when we use these classes to describe young stellar populations.

Disentangling variability with colour and disk class

A light curve tells us how a source changes in brightness; its path in a colour–magnitude diagram helps us ask why. I compared these slopes with simple models for extinction and stellar spots, then used the disk classifications to interpret them. Sources with disks and extinction-like slopes are consistent with variable obscuration. Shallower slopes in disk-bearing sources point towards hot spots and variable accretion, while diskless sources can be explained by hot spots. These are clues to the dominant mechanism: more than one process can contribute to an individual light curve.
