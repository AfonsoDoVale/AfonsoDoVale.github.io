The observations behind the catalogue

At about 189 pc and roughly 1–3 Myr old, Cha-I is a nearby laboratory for the planetary-mass population. Our catalogue combines deep ground-based optical and near-infrared imaging with Spitzer and WISE mid-infrared photometry. The X-shooter proposal describes approximately 36 photometric bands/instrument combinations and a roughly 25-year astrometric baseline. Combining archival epochs measures motions for faint, red sources beyond Gaia’s kinematic coverage. Deep stacks provide complementary views of the faintest sources.

Good candidates, then spectra

The probabilistic random forest initially identifies about 200 high-probability candidates. Photometric and kinematic checks produce smaller follow-up samples: ten high-priority targets in the X-shooter programme and twenty in the SOAR/TripleSpec proposal, including seven with estimated planetary masses. These estimates remain conditional on membership. Spectra will test youth through low-gravity features and the H-band shape, determine spectral types and extinction, and distinguish genuinely cool members from reddened contaminants.

Project abstract · working draft

We assemble a multi-epoch optical-to-mid-infrared catalogue of Chamaeleon I to search for its faintest members. Imaging spanning approximately 20–25 years provides proper motions for about 1.5 million sources, including more than 400,000 without Gaia DR3 kinematic solutions. A probabilistic random-forest classifier trained on known members and field sources combines astrometry and photometry to identify promising substellar candidates. We refine the selection using colour–magnitude diagrams, proper-motion consistency and spectral energy distributions. The outcome is a high-quality candidate sample for follow-up spectroscopy to establish youth, spectral type and membership. This work has not yet determined an IMF for Cha-I.
